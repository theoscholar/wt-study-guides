Introduction
Heart of it: We obey Jehovah mainly because we love him — obedience from the heart, at all times, not only when watched or when we see the benefit.
Paragraph 1 p. 8

Why might people obey those in authority?

Open paragraph 1

PEOPLE obey those in authority for a variety of reasons. Many might do so only if they are being watched or if they want to avoid punishment. When it comes to obeying God, some make a show of obedience to gain prestige, as did the religious leaders in Jesus’ day. (Matt. 23:5) In contrast, we obey Jehovah because we see the benefits of his guidance. (Isa. 48:17, 18) That is a fine motive for showing obedience, but we have an even more important reason to obey him.

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 8, par. 1.

Main Point: People may obey to avoid punishment, to be watched, or to gain prestige, as the religious leaders did. We obey Jehovah because we see the benefits of his guidance, but our main reason is deeper still.

Additional Point: Matthew 23:5 says the leaders did their works “to be seen by men,” broadening their scripture-containing cases and lengthening their fringes — obedience as display. Isaiah 48:17, 18 adds that Jehovah teaches us “to benefit” ourselves, promising peace “like a river” if we pay attention.

Why the scriptures matter: The scriptures set two motives side by side: obedience performed to be seen by men, and obedience that flows from Jehovah’s guidance and its benefits.

Matthew 23:5

5 All the works they do, they do to be seen by men, for they broaden the scripture-containing cases that they wear as safeguards and lengthen the fringes of their garments.

Isaiah 48:17, 18

17 This is what Jehovah says, your Repurchaser, the Holy One of Israel: “I, Jehovah, am your God, The One teaching you to benefit yourself, The One guiding you in the way you should walk.

18 If only you would pay attention to my commandments! Then your peace would become just like a river And your righteousness like the waves of the sea.

Paragraph 2 p. 8

What is our main reason for obeying Jehovah?

Open paragraph 2

2 The main reason we obey Jehovah is that we love him deeply. (Matt. 22:37, 38) Our love moves us to be “obedient from the heart.” (Rom. 6:17) Love for Jehovah and “an obedient heart” motivate us to do our best to observe his commands not just when others are watching or when we see the immediate benefits of doing so but at all times. Admittedly, our heart is treacherous, so maintaining the right attitude can at times be a challenge. In this article, we will look at two Bible accounts that can help us to develop an obedient heart. First, we will consider the time when Jehovah delivered the Israelites from Egypt. Then, we will look at the fine example of King Jehoshaphat of Judah.

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 8, par. 2.

Main Point: Our main reason is deep love for Jehovah. That love moves us to be “obedient from the heart,” observing his commands at all times — not only when others watch or when we see immediate benefits. Our heart is treacherous, so keeping that attitude takes effort.

Additional Point: Romans 6:17 speaks of those once “slaves of sin” who “became obedient from the heart to that pattern of teaching” — obedience as a change of master, not merely of behaviour. Matthew 22:37, 38 calls love for Jehovah the “greatest and first commandment,” with whole heart, soul, and mind.

Why the scriptures matter: Love is named the greatest commandment, and Romans 6:17 shows what it produces: obedience “from the heart,” not only when watched.

First Obedient and Then Rebellious
Heart of it: The Israelites obeyed at the Passover, then rebelled at the Red Sea when fear replaced trust.
Paragraph 3 pp. 8–9

How may Jehovah’s command to splash blood on the doorposts have tested the Israelites’ obedience? (See also picture.)

Open paragraph 3

3 Before Jehovah delivered the Israelites from Egypt, he gave them a command that tested their obedience. To avoid the tenth and final plague, they were to sacrifice a ram or a male goat and splash its blood on the doorposts of their houses. (Ex. 12:5, 7) Jehovah would pass over the dwellings marked with the blood and spare the firstborn of man and of beast of each household. However, the Israelites could have been tempted to disobey. Why? They might have feared retaliation from the Egyptians, who considered rams to be sacred. (Ex. 8:25-27) Still, the Israelites saw the urgent need for obedience and “did just as Jehovah had commanded.” (Ex. 12:28) Jehovah blessed them by sparing their firstborn.

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” pp. 8–9, par. 3.

Main Point: Jehovah told them to sacrifice a ram or male goat and splash its blood on the doorposts so the firstborn would be spared. That tested them because Egyptians held rams sacred, and they might have feared retaliation. Still, they “did just as Jehovah had commanded.”

Additional Point: Exodus 8:26 records Moses’ warning that sacrificing what Egyptians found detestable “right before their eyes” could mean stoning — the danger was real, not imagined. Exodus 12:28 records that the Israelites “did just as Jehovah had commanded Moses and Aaron,” showing obedience worked through Jehovah’s appointed representatives.

Why the scriptures matter: The Passover instructions and the Egyptians’ regard for rams show why the command was a real test — and Exodus 12:28 records that the Israelites passed it.

Paragraph 4 p. 9

What unusual instructions did Jehovah give the Israelites on their way out of Egypt?

Open paragraph 4

4 That very night, the Israelites left Egypt. Jehovah first led them toward the northern end of the Red Sea, but then he gave them instructions that defied human logic. He told them to turn south and encamp between the sea and the mountains. At that campsite, it seemed that they would be trapped between Pharaoh’s army that was pursuing them and the Red Sea. Yet, the Israelites once again did as Jehovah had commanded through his servant Moses. (Ex. 14:1-4) But when they saw the Egyptians approaching, they “became terrified” and even accused Moses of leading them to their death! (Ex. 14:5-11) Why did they begin regretting their decision to obey? And how did Jehovah view their attitude?

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 9, par. 4.

Main Point: After leaving Egypt, Jehovah told them to turn south and camp between the sea and the mountains, where they seemed trapped between Pharaoh’s army and the Red Sea. They obeyed, but when they saw the Egyptians approaching, they became terrified and accused Moses of leading them to their death.

Additional Point: Exodus 14:3, 4 shows Jehovah foresaw Pharaoh’s thinking — “They are wandering in confusion” — and said, “I will glorify myself by means of Pharaoh.” The trap was deliberate: Jehovah used the very position that frightened Israel to make his name known to Egypt.

Why the scriptures matter: Exodus 14 records both the instruction and the reaction: Jehovah’s direction seemed to trap them, and fear quickly turned to accusation.

Paragraph 5 pp. 9–10

What did the Israelites fail to do at the Red Sea? (Psalm 106:7 and footnote)

Open paragraph 5

5 The Bible says that at the Red Sea, the Israelites “did not appreciate [Jehovah’s] wonderful works” in Egypt or “remember” his loyal love. (Read Psalm 106:7 and footnote.) Discerning their heart condition, Jehovah said that the people “rebelled . . . by the Red Sea.” Even though they followed Moses through the Red Sea, they never corrected their attitude. Jehovah ultimately rejected that entire generation of Israelites and did not allow them to enter the Promised Land. (Ps. 106:21, 24-26) What can we learn from their experience?

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” pp. 9–10, par. 5.

Paragraph 6 p. 10

What will an obedient heart enable us to do? (Jeremiah 23:4)

Open paragraph 6

6 The Exodus account shows that we need to examine our reasons for obeying Jehovah and those he assigns to lead his people. Do we obey only when we see some immediate benefits? It’s possible that some of the Israelites sprinkled the ram’s blood on the doorposts simply to save their firstborn. Also, does our obedience depend on what others do? On the night of the Passover, Jehovah gave the Israelites “favor in the eyes of the Egyptians.” (Ex. 12:36) But when the Egyptians later turned against them, the Israelites lost their trust in Jehovah and rebelled. (Ex. 14:10-12) In contrast to their attitude, if we have an obedient heart, we will be moved to trust in Jehovah and in those he has raised up to take the lead. (Read Jeremiah 23:4.) We will follow the direction we receive even if we do not fully understand all the reasons for it or if doing so angers those who hate Jehovah. How can we avoid making the same mistakes the Israelites made and develop an obedient heart?

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 10, par. 6.

Main Point: An obedient heart will move us to trust Jehovah and those he has raised up to take the lead, following direction even when we do not fully understand it or when obeying angers those who hate Jehovah. We must examine whether our obedience depends on immediate benefits or on what others do.

Additional Point: Jeremiah 23:4 promises shepherds who “will really shepherd” the flock, so that they “will no longer be afraid” and “none will be missing.” Trusting appointed shepherds is therefore trusting Jehovah’s own provision — he raises them up precisely so his people need not fear.

Why the scriptures matter: Exodus 12:36 and 14:10-12 show obedience that rose and fell with circumstances; Jeremiah 23:4 points to the shepherds Jehovah raises up so his people need not fear.

How did King Jehoshaphat feel when he was threatened by invading nations?

Open paragraph 10

12 Build your Bible knowledge. Jehoshaphat’s prayer revealed his depth of knowledge. He understood how Israel had inherited its land through God’s promise to Abraham. (Gen. 12:7) Jehoshaphat’s words were similar to those Solomon had uttered when praying at the inauguration of the temple. (2 Chron. 6:28-30; 20:5-9) Jehoshaphat’s knowledge strengthened his trust in Jehovah and his resolve to obey Him. To strengthen our resolve, we need to keep building our “treasure store” by adding new truths to what we already know. (Matt. 13:52) As we do, we strengthen our determination to obey Jehovah even during challenging situations.

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 12, par. 12.

Main Point: Build Bible knowledge. Jehoshaphat’s prayer showed depth: he knew how Israel inherited its land through God’s promise to Abraham, and his words echoed Solomon’s temple prayer. Knowledge strengthened his trust and resolve, and adding new truths to our “treasure store” does the same for us.

Additional Point: Matthew 13:52 pictures the taught instructor bringing “out of his treasure store things both new and old” — knowledge grows by addition, not replacement. Genesis 12:7 records the promise itself, “To your offspring I am going to give this land,” the very history Jehoshaphat recalled in prayer.

Why the scriptures matter: The prayer itself proves the point: Jehoshaphat could draw on God’s promise to Abraham and on Solomon’s words because he knew them.

How can humility help us to be obedient?

Open paragraph 13

13 Cultivate humility. Jehoshaphat’s prayer also reflected his humility. As king, he could have been tempted to rely on his strength and ability to handle the threat. Humility will likewise help us to maintain an obedient heart in any circumstance.—Phil. 2:5, 8.

— The Watchtower, September 2026, “How to Develop ‘an Obedient Heart’,” p. 12, par. 13.

Main Point: Jehoshaphat’s prayer reflected humility. As king he could have relied on his own strength, but he publicly acknowledged that Judah needed God’s help. That humble attitude made accepting God’s direction easier, and humility will likewise help us keep an obedient heart in any circumstance.

How did Jehoshaphat respond to God’s instructions?

Open paragraph 14

14 Consider how your actions affect others. Jehovah answered Jehoshaphat’s prayer by giving him clear instructions through the prophet Jahaziel, who told the people that they would not need to fight. How Would You Answer?
Heart of it: Two examples, one lesson — keep an obedient heart.

How would you answer?

  1. What can we learn from the Israelites’ warning example during their Exodus from Egypt?
    They obeyed at the Passover, sprinkling the blood as commanded, but at the Red Sea they did not appreciate Jehovah’s wonderful works or remember his loyal love, and they rebelled. We learn to obey from love and trust, not only when we see immediate benefits or when others support us. (¶3–6)
  2. How did Jehoshaphat show that he had “an obedient heart”?
    He resolved to search for Jehovah, prayed humbly, drew on Bible knowledge, listened to Jehovah’s direction through Jahaziel, consulted with the people, and sent singers ahead of the armed men in faith. (¶11–15)
  3. What can we learn from Jehoshaphat’s example?
    To strengthen our love, Bible knowledge, and humility; to obey even when direction seems unusual; and, for those in authority, to consult others and encourage obedience by word and deed. (¶7, 12–13, 16)
