PARAGRAPH 4w26.09
How do the opening words of Psalm 91 assure us of Jehovah’s protection? (Psalm 91:1, 2)
Open paragraph 4

The basic message of Psalm 91 can be summed up in a few words: Trust in God’s protection. From the opening two verses, we are assured that Jehovah will protect those who put their trust in him. (Read Psalm 91:1, 2.) What could make us feel more secure than knowing that we are under the protective shadow and wings of the Most High, the Almighty? (Ruth 2:12; Ps. 17:8) The nouns “refuge” and “stronghold” remind us of the protection that a walled city provided in Bible times. The city’s inhabitants were protected within its walls, and those in outlying areas could run into the city to be safe from enemies. In the verses that follow, the psalmist uses vivid imagery to assure us that Jehovah can protect His people no matter what dangers they may face.—Ps. 91:3-13.

The Watchtower, September 2026 Study Edition (w26.09), paragraph 4.

Main Point: Psalm 91 opens by calling Jehovah our refuge and stronghold. Like a walled city, he shelters those who run to him and trust him. His shadow and wings picture close, personal care, assuring us he can protect us from any danger.

Additional Point: “Refuge” and “stronghold” are not feelings; they are places to run to. Ruth 2:12 uses the same wing imagery for a foreigner seeking shelter, showing that protection in this psalm is for anyone who chooses to dwell close to Jehovah, not only for the naturally strong.

Why the scriptures matter: Psalm 91:1, 2 is marked “Read” in the article; Ruth 2:12 and Psalm 17:8 use the same shadow-and-wings picture, confirming that the opening verses promise shelter, not distance.

Why the scriptures matter: Joshua 23:14 testifies that not one of Jehovah’s promises has failed, and Numbers 23:19 says God cannot lie, so the “I will” promises of Psalm 91:14-16 carry full weight.

What Kind of Protection Does Psalm 91 Promise?
Heart of it: Psalm 91 promises complete protection — spiritual, physical, and emotional.
PARAGRAPH 7w26.09
What kind of protection does Psalm 91 promise?
Open paragraph 7

As our publications have explained, Psalm 91 is a promise of spiritual security, or protection. We can be sure that Jehovah will always provide what we need so that we can protect ourselves from spiritual dangers and safeguard our precious friendship with him. But does this psalm promise only spiritual protection? No. This psalm is also a promise that Jehovah will protect his people as a group from physical and emotional harm at the hands of their enemies. Why can we say that Psalm 91 also applies to physical and emotional protection?

The Watchtower, September 2026 Study Edition (w26.09), paragraph 7.

Main Point: Psalm 91 promises spiritual protection — Jehovah always provides what we need to guard our friendship with him from spiritual danger. It also promises that he will protect his people as a group from physical and emotional harm from their enemies.

Additional Point: Note the careful limit: the group promise is about Jehovah’s people as a whole surviving their enemies, not a guarantee that no individual will ever suffer. That distinction, developed in paragraphs 16 and 17, keeps the promise both strong and honest.

Why the scriptures matter: The paragraph itself is the source for the twofold promise; the next two paragraphs prove the physical and emotional side from Jesus’ own use of the psalm.

PARAGRAPH 8-9w26.09
How did Jesus respond to one of Satan’s temptations, and what can we learn from his response? (Matthew 4:5-7)
Open paragraph 8

Consider how Jesus responded when Satan tempted him and quoted the words found in Psalm 91. (Read Matthew 4:5-7.) Referring to the words of Psalm 91:11, 12 as a promise of physical protection, Satan tempted Jesus to jump off the temple battlement to see if angels would protect him from harm. How did Jesus respond? He did not say: ‘You are mistaken, Satan! That psalm does not promise physical protection.’ Rather, Jesus replied: “You must not put Jehovah your God to the test.” What can we learn from Jesus’ response?

The Watchtower, September 2026 Study Edition (w26.09), paragraph 8.

Open paragraph 9

Jesus was fully aware that his Father had at times used angels to protect His people from physical harm at the hands of their enemies. (2 Ki. 6:15-17; 19:34, 35; Dan. 3:19, 20, 26-28; 6:16-22) But Jesus refused to test his Father by expecting angels to protect him from physical harm if he behaved recklessly. So Satan misused Psalm 91, not by referring to it as a promise of physical protection, but by using it to justify putting Jehovah to the test.

The Watchtower, September 2026 Study Edition (w26.09), paragraph 9.

Main Point: Satan quoted Psalm 91:11, 12 to urge Jesus to jump from the temple and expect angelic rescue. Jesus did not deny that the psalm promises physical protection; he replied, “You must not put Jehovah your God to the test.” Satan’s misuse was in urging reckless testing of God.

Additional Point: Jesus’ silence is evidence: if Psalm 91 promised only spiritual safety, that would have been the easy correction. Instead he corrected the use, not the promise — showing the psalm does speak of physical protection, but never as a licence to act recklessly and demand a rescue.

Why the scriptures matter: Matthew 4:5-7 is marked “Read”; the angelic rescues in 2 Kings 6 and Daniel 3 and 6 show Jehovah has indeed protected his people physically, without being tested.

We do not need to fear the coming destruction of this wicked world in the war of Armageddon. Why not? We trust in Jehovah as our refuge and our stronghold. (Jer. 25:33; Zech. 14:12) But during that war, not one of God’s precious sheep will be killed at the hands of his enemies—human or demonic.—Luke 21:28; 2 Pet. 2:9.

Open paragraph 20

During the war of Armageddon, the psalmist’s inspired words may literally prove true: “A thousand will fall at your side and ten thousand at your right hand, but to you it will not come near. You will only see it with your eyes as you witness the punishment of the wicked. Because you said: ‘Jehovah is my refuge,’ you have made the Most High your dwelling.” (Ps. 91:7-9) Once Armageddon begins, we can expect that Jehovah will provide complete physical, emotional, and spiritual protection for his people not just as a group but also as individuals. Because you say: “Jehovah is my refuge,” Jehovah promises: “To you it will not come near.” Let us, then, face the future with confidence, knowing that we can fully trust in Jehovah for protection.

HOW WOULD YOU ANSWER?

  1. What kind of protection does Psalm 91 promise? — Spiritual protection always, and protection for God’s people as a group from physical and emotional harm; complete individual protection once Armageddon begins.
  2. How is Jehovah protecting us now? — Spiritually, through his Word, his holy spirit, and his organization; and as a group, through his angels, so pure worship continues — though individuals may still suffer.
  3. How will Jehovah protect us in the future? — He will not allow his people to be exterminated in the great tribulation, and at Armageddon he will protect them completely, physically, emotionally, and spiritually.
